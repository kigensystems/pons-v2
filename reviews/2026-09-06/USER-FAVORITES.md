## Visual observations

All five original sites were opened and visually sampled in Chrome. This was a focused first-view and selected-scene review, not an exhaustive capture or usability audit.

- **Shader:** Cream serif lettering with luminous edges, slight color separation, dark haze, and beige computer hardware. Selected Work becomes a curved filmstrip inside a screen-like frame. About switches to staged office photography and cream editorial columns while retaining the visual treatment. The effect extends through navigation and text; it is more than a background behind ordinary HTML.
- **Oxigen:** A dark teal landscape made from small cubical units, with a palm assembled from the same vocabulary. On scrolling, parts of the tree become luminous outlines. Foreground solidity, distant haze, and incomplete structure create the sensation of a reconstructed place. Its compact wordmark stays much simpler than the scene.
- **PX PUSH:** Blue sky, foggy clouds, an extruded rotating mark, large sans-serif typography, scanlines, and dark rounded screen edges. The monitor treatment continues into gray text sections. This is a different retro expression from Shader's glowing serif and office-photograph mixture.
- **Pear:** Saturated blue, an illustrated classical figure emerging from a curtain, elegant serif type, small technical labels, fine framing rules, and conspicuous print-like texture. Scroll changes the framing and reveals close fabric detail. Its appeal can inform texture and composition without importing the classical subject matter.
- **Exebenus:** Black, orange, wireframe machinery, depth gauges, restrained navigation, and paired drilling views. The spatial scene has an explanatory subject. The loaded hero and a subsequent two-path comparison were visually inspected; later outcomes were read in page text and creator documentation, not all visually replayed.

Interpretation: the shared attraction is imagery seen through an imperfect or reconstructed medium. Useful working vocabulary is **atmospheric retrofuturism**, **retrocomputing**, **CRT/analog-video treatment**, **voxel landscapes**, and **technical wireframe visualization**. These terms describe overlapping qualities, not one standardized style or a mandatory effect bundle.

## Technology: established versus inferred

**Oxigen:** Its inspected Awwwards submission explicitly describes voxels and lists Three.js and WebGL. The visible cube-based geometry agrees with that description. Calling it a voxel landscape is more precise than calling it a point cloud. The exact production shader pipeline was not audited. [Submission](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/oxigen).

**Shader:** Its creator documents Three.js, React Three Fiber, TSL, and a WebGPU pipeline with WebGL compatibility. The account names film grain, chromatic aberration, and bloom, and identifies 1980s business technology as the creative influence. These are first-party implementation claims; current fallback behavior was not tested. [Creator's case study](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/05/19/80s-business-tech-seamless-scene-transitions-inside-shader-ses-scroll-driven-webgpu-pipeline/).

**PX PUSH:** The creator distinguishes CSS/image-based CRT overlays from separate Three.js scenes. Its cloud scene uses planes and distance fog, so a hazy appearance does not imply voxel geometry. [Creator's case study](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/08/07/the-department-is-open-building-the-px-push-website/).

**Pear:** The inspected submission lists React, JavaScript, and Tailwind. These tags do not establish how the film, texture, or compositing was implemented. [Submission](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/pear).

**Exebenus:** Refokus credits Webflow development and 3D animation. That does not establish live WebGL or Three.js. Its case study explains the comparison between operating conditions with and without better visibility. [Refokus case study](https://www.refokus.com/projects/exebenus).

WebGL/WebGPU are browser graphics APIs; Three.js is a library that can work with those rendering backends. They are not competing names for a visual style. Likewise, voxels, points, wireframes, fog, grain, and glow describe different aspects of a scene.

## Implications for our own identity

Treat Shader as the lead mood reference because the user explicitly selected it as the favorite. Oxigen is especially useful for exploring spatial representations of information. The remaining sites provide supporting ideas about type, texture, logo behavior, and explanatory sequencing.

The visual brief should allow a more expressive, atmospheric direction than the restrained options in the initial review. Functional clarity remains useful, but it should not flatten the preferred character into a conventional infrastructure homepage. These preferences also directly challenge the Gemini report's blanket rejection of playfulness.

A promising exploration would give an original symbol a clear flat form and a richer rendered or animated expression. The surrounding world could reveal structure, connections, or activity using the same visual logic. Actual task controls and important values should remain comfortable to read; atmospheric effects can vary in intensity across the experience.

Avoid mechanically combining Shader's computer, Oxigen's palm, Pear's classical imagery, and Exebenus's machinery. Their subjects belong to their own stories. Find a subject and visual behavior specific to our complementary Pons product. The user has not yet chosen what that subject is.

No design was built or selected during this review. Browser observations establish that sampled scenes rendered here; they do not establish mobile support, accessibility, performance, or end-to-end product functionality. No source research or AGENTS.md guidance was changed.
